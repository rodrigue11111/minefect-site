Context & challenges

Mineral exploration determines the viability and value of a deposit. The quality of geological data collected — directly dependent on the consumables and equipment used — underpins investment decisions that can reach hundreds of millions of dollars. Across Sub-Saharan Africa, exploration campaigns often take place in remote areas with no infrastructure, greatly complicating logistics and supply chain management.

MINEFECT understands the stakes of exploration: every metre drilled represents a significant cost, and core quality determines geological model accuracy. We support you with drilling consumables optimised for your geological formation, reliable instrumentation, and logistics capable of reaching the most isolated sites on the continent.

Key sector challenges

The constraints we help you overcome

Core quality and recovery

Recovering intact, representative cores is the primary objective of exploration drilling. A poor choice of core barrel, bit, or drilling fluid can cause core disintegration, distorting the geological interpretation and forcing costly re-drilling of the same intervals.

Logistics in remote areas

Exploration campaigns often take place in regions with no paved roads, far from any warehouse. A consumable stockout (bits, rods, drilling fluids) can halt an entire campaign for weeks, triggering massive mobilisation and demobilisation costs that dwarf the cost of the missing supplies.

Drill string durability

Rods, bits, and barrels are subjected to extreme mechanical stresses, amplified by geological variation (hard-soft transitions, fractured zones, aquifers). A rod failure at depth can cause a costly lost-in-hole situation and force hole abandonment.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the most common questions from our clients

Bit selection depends primarily on rock hardness (RQD, compressive strength) and abrasivity. For soft to medium formations (limestone, marl), wide-matrix coarse-diamond bits are favoured for fast penetration. For hard to very hard formations (quartzite, granite), fine-matrix fine-diamond bits provide better wear resistance. Fractured formations require double-tube systems to prevent core loss. Our technical team can help you select based on your site's geological data.

For depths up to 300 m, NQ (47.6 mm core diameter) is often sufficient. Between 300 and 800 m, HQ (63.5 mm core) offers the best recovery-cost trade-off. For deep holes (800 m+) where core recovery is a priority, PQ (85 mm core) is recommended. It is also common to start in PQ and reduce to HQ or NQ (coring-in-core) depending on formations encountered.

In the event of a lost drill string (broken rod, stuck string), the first step is to assess recoverability with an overshot tool. We supply these fishing tools and retrieving rods.
